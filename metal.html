Metal

Metal is the element of reflection — the reflection of life itself. It is what appears in a mirror: a virtual life, an image that is real and yet not the thing it shows. Metal does not create; it reveals. Its season is autumn, its direction is the West — the place where the sun goes down, where things end so that something else may begin.

In a person, Metal corresponds to sadness — the quiet, clear feeling that comes when something is finished. It can be a bright premonition of a new cycle, the gentle melancholy of letting go. Or it can be the terrible grief of loss, the weight that does not lift. The same emotion, in two different states: the sadness that opens, and the sadness that closes.

Metal is the capacity for parting. When in balance, a person does not cling to their baggage — they part easily with what they have gathered, without sentiment and without regret. Metal knows that everything that is held must one day be released. But when Metal loses its balance, the opposite appears: excessive attachment, an inability to overcome grief, a refusal to let go of what has already left. This is the shadow of Metal — not sharpness, but rigidity.

Metal is tied to the Lungs and Large Intestine — the organs that take in and release, that receive the breath and let go of what is no longer needed. Its planet is Venus, the bright evening star, the planet of beauty and value. Its taste is pungent, the taste that sharpens and opens. Its color is grey-white — the color of the polished surface, the color of reflection itself, the color of things that have been distilled to their essence.
