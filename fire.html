Fire

Fire is the element of transformation. It is the only one of the five that is not a physical body but a process — a movement, a change, an event. Fire does not exist as a thing; it exists as an action. It spreads upward, always rising, always consuming, always turning one thing into another. Its season is summer — the peak of the year, when everything burns with life.

In a person, Fire corresponds to joy — the open, radiant state of the heart. It is the warmth that connects people, the spark of recognition, the light that makes the world visible. When balanced, it becomes presence, clarity, and the capacity to love without clinging. Fire gives, and in giving, it shines.

But when Fire loses its balance, joy turns into something darker. Excessive Fire produces a manic glee — a feverish, hollow elation that feeds on itself and cannot be satisfied. And it is fed by the rage of Wood: anger that has not been transformed becomes fuel for a fire that burns without purpose. This is the shadow of Fire — not warmth, but wildfire.

Fire is tied to the Heart and Small Intestine — the organs of spirit and discernment. Its planet is Mars, the red wanderer, the planet of war. Its taste is bitter, the taste that descends and dries. Its color is red — the brightest of all, the color that cannot be ignored.
